ABSTRACT
Molecular optimization must improve target activity and satisfy developability constraints
within limited evaluation budgets. Classical methods require tailored rules or training to
incorporate chemical instructions and property feedback. Frozen language models can con-
dition edits on this information, but need explicit constraint control and relevant experience.
We therefore present P HARM AGENT , a constraint-aware molecular search method driven
by adaptive external state. Its Lagrangian controller translates violations in accepted states
into accumulated constraint pressure, keeping this history separate from current property
measurements. Structure-indexed replay complements this feedback with relevant evalu-
ated transitions that guide subsequent proposals. As a curriculum progressively activates
constraints, candidates and the incumbent are compared under the same current objective,
and the accepted state determines the next multiplier update. We derive an exact identity
that characterizes how accepted-state violations accumulate in the controller’s multipliers.
Across ﬁve tasks with ﬁve independent runs, PHARM AGENT achieves a summed area under
the target-score curves (AUC) of 3.9208 in target-only search, improving over MOLLEO
by 37.3%. With online constraints, it achieves a property-adjusted AUC of 0.7076, improv-
ing over the strongest online baseline, ExLLM, by 53.8%. These results rank ﬁrst among
all evaluated methods in both target-only and constraint-aware search. The online compar-
ison covers all ﬁve baseline frameworks. The full system leads every ablation variant in
target quality, property-adjusted performance, and Pareto hypervolume. All ﬁve molecu-
lar cases reach feasible ﬁnal states, documenting target gains and trade-offs. These results
establish P HARM AGENT as a powerful framework for budget-limited molecular optimiza-
tion, achieving leading aggregate performance through adaptive external search state and
frozen language models.

1 I NTRODUCTION
Molecular optimization searches for structures with improved target activity and suitable physicochemical,
synthesizability, and absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity (ADMET) properties (Bick-
erton et al. , 2012; Gao & Coley , 2020). Under limited budgets, each evaluation should guide productive
structural edits ( Gao et al. , 2022). Graph-GA ’s stochastic mutation and crossover can spend evaluations on
unproductive edits ( Jensen, 2019; Wang et al., 2025). REINVENT4 adapts its policy through reward-driven
parameter updates ( Loefﬂer et al. , 2024), while latent-space Bayesian optimization depends on learned rep-
resentations and valid decoding ( Gómez-Bombarelli et al. , 2018). These methods require translating chem-
ical instructions and heterogeneous feedback into search rules or training signals ( Ran et al. , 2025). Large
language models (LLMs) can instead condition chemically informed edits directly on task instructions and
evaluated experience ( Wang et al. , 2025; Ran et al. , 2025; Krüger et al. , 2026). Freezing their parameters
enables adaptation through context without retraining ( Y ang et al., 2024; M. Bran et al. , 2024).
However, proposal quality alone leaves two feedback gaps (Figure 1(a)). First, target gains can introduce
property violations (Gao & Coley, 2020). Persistent violations call for search priorities that adapt to violation
history, beyond ﬁxed penalty weights or post-hoc ﬁltering. Second, multi-round interaction creates experi-
ence, but useful guidance requires edits relevant to the current molecular structure ( Shinn et al. , 2023; Liu
et al., 2025). These gaps motivate dynamic constraint control and structure-aware experience retrieval.
To address both gaps, we propose P HARM AGENT , a constraint-aware molecular search method that adapts
external state around frozen language models. Generator and Reviewer execute the proposal, evaluation, ac-
ceptance, and feedback loop with scientiﬁc tools and medicinal-chemistry retrieval-augmented generation
(MedChem-RAG) (Figure 1(b)). Three mechanisms organize external state (Figure 1(c)). A Lagrangian con-
troller accumulates accepted-state violations as historical pressure, separate from current measurements (Bert-
sekas, 1999). Chemical-aware contextual experience replay (Chemical-CER) retrieves structurally related
evaluated edits, and a curriculum introduces constraints after target exploration. Candidates and the incum-
bent share the current objective, while stored attempts remain distinct from accepted states. Table 1 compares
the evaluated conﬁgurations.

Multipliers start at zero, making nonnegative projection inactive. Inactive multipliers remain unchanged.
When a proposal is rejected, the retained incumbent drives the update. Thus the accumulated pressure reﬂects
the search state that persists into the next round, while rejected attempts remain available to replay. Empty
rounds skip updates. Active-set expansion resets ρ to ρ0 > 0 before rescoring, retaining multipliers.
The current-status block reports each active property’s value, threshold, direction, signed margin, and viola-
tion. The history block reports λi, accumulated from accepted-state violations. These blocks answer different
questions. Measurements determine which constraints the current molecule satisﬁes, while multiplier history
identiﬁes constraints that have repeatedly resisted the search. A feasible molecule can retain a large multiplier
because earlier accepted states violated that constraint. This pressure increases the cost of reintroducing the
violation in future candidates. Conversely, zero pressure alone does not establish feasibility. The Reviewer
receives each candidate’s own measurements together with this history, allowing proposal guidance to use
both present satisfaction and persistent constraint priorities.
3.4 S TRUCTURE -I NDEXED EXPERIENCE REPLAY
Chemical-CER retrieves structurally relevant attempts using source Morgan ﬁngerprints (radius 2, 2048
bits) ( Rogers & Hahn , 2010). A completed round records incoming incumbent y(t), round-best candidate
c(t), target/ADMET changes, and the scalar change under the collection-time objective. Replay stores the
evaluated attempt (y(t), c(t)), and the acceptance rule tracks (y(t), ¯x(t)). The stored scalar delta retains the
collection-time objective.

3.5 C ONSTRAINT SCHEDULING
A ﬁxed target-ﬁrst curriculum ( Hou, 2026) activates no ADMET constraints in rounds [0, 20),
MW/logP/QED/SA in [20, 60), and all seven in [60,∞). Round indices determine the constraint-activation
boundaries. Expansion adds measurements to evaluation and context, resetting quadratic pressure while re-
taining multipliers. Stagnation triggers bounded context recovery with the same activation schedule and
acceptance rule (Appendix H). A continuous sigmoid-based ADMET score supports replay annotation and
Pareto evaluation (Eq. A1). Hard feasibility requires all seven inequalities, and acceptance uses Eq. 3.

4.4 M ECHANISM ABLATIONS
The full system outperforms all six variants on all three aggregate metrics (Table 4). Removing Lagrangian
feedback reduces AA-AUC by 32.4%, compared with a 1.1% reduction in target-AUC sum. The loss is con-
centrated in feasible-solution progress. Removing CER reduces target-AUC sum by 7.9% and AA-AUC by
22.9%. Removing Curriculum reduces them by 13.4% and 11.5%, and PHV by 11.6%. MedChem-RAG re-
moval produces the largest target-AUC reduction among component removals (18.8%) and lowers AA-AUC
by 41.4%. Reviewer removal lowers AA-AUC by 21.3%. The full system reaches 0.7076 AA-AUC versus
0.3000 for the single-agent conﬁguration. The distinct loss proﬁles reveal complementary contributions from
constraint feedback, structural experience, scheduling, chemical priors, and review.
4.5 M OLECULAR CASES OF PROPERTY -V IOLATION RESOLUTION
Figure 4(a–e) compares property proﬁles before full constraint activation (round 59) and at termination, us-
ing run 0 uniformly across tasks. Figure 4(f) shows the corresponding GSK3 β structures. P HARM AGENT
resolves property violations in all ﬁve illustrated trajectories. Every earlier state violates at least one of the
ﬁnal seven requirements, and every ﬁnal state satisﬁes all seven. Target scores increase for GSK3 β (0.20 to
0.92) and Sitagliptin (0.1443 to 0.4533), remain at 1.00 for Albuterol, and decrease for JNK3 (1.00 to 0.86)
and DRD2 (1.0000 to 0.9864). For GSK3 β, the predicted hERG block score falls from 0.5754 to 0.3496
(bound 0.5), while Caco-2 increases from −5.5022 to−4.4525 (bound−5.15). Appendix C provides all
structure pairs, raw properties, and the selection protocol.
5 D ISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
PHARM AGENT achieves leading aggregate performance through adaptive external search state and frozen
language models. E1 leads aggregate target search, and E2 outperforms all six online strategies on all ﬁve
aggregate metrics. The Lagrangian controller gives constraint history an explicit role in acceptance and
proposal feedback, while Chemical-CER and the curriculum supply relevant measured experience and coor-
dinate constraint activation. Removing Lagrangian feedback lowers AA-AUC by 32.4%. Every component
removal lowers all three aggregate metrics, and all ﬁve molecular cases resolve their property violations. To-
gether, these results establish adaptive constraint feedback and structured chemical experience as a powerful
approach to molecular search with frozen models.
Limitations. Evaluation covers ﬁve tasks and predicted properties. Multiplier boundedness remains an
assumption of the appendix analysis, and ablation uncertainty is unavailable. Appendix M summarizes the
few task-level exceptions, validation needs, and computational-cost considerations.

experiments use the request and round limits speciﬁed here.

J B ROADER IMPACT
PHARM AGENT is a computational tool for molecular optimization and does not directly produce physical
molecules. Its outputs are SMILES strings that must be validated by medicinal chemists and tested in wet-lab
assays before entering any drug development pipeline. By incorporating ADMET constraints, the framework
is designed to steer optimization toward molecules with better safety proﬁles, which could reduce the number
of toxic candidates that proceed to animal testing.
A potential risk of any molecular generation system is the production of molecules with harmful properties
(toxins, controlled substances, environmental pollutants). The implemented screen penalizes predicted hERG
inhibition. It does not include genotoxicity endpoints or establish safety against these broader risks. Nonethe-
less, the system is not a substitute for regulatory toxicology assessment, and users should apply additional
safety checks before any experimental synthesis.
The ADMET-PMO benchmark itself poses minimal risk, as it consists of computational oracle functions
applied to virtual molecules. Releasing the benchmark and evaluation code promotes transparency and repro-
ducibility in the ﬁeld.
